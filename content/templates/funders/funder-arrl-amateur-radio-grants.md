Every rule below is quoted from the ARRL Amateur Radio Grants page cited above. This is **not**
the ARRL Club Grant Program — that is a separate programme with a separate application, and its
cycle is unresolved. Do not carry a date from one to the other.

## What this programme refuses

Three exclusions decide most applications before anyone reads the narrative.

**Emergency communications.** "Grant requests for emergency communications equipment, facilities,
or projects will not be considered." A proposal built around ARES, RACES, SKYWARN or disaster
response is at the wrong programme. If the project is genuinely about preparedness, rewrite it
around what it teaches and who it licenses, or apply elsewhere — do not disguise it, because the
reviewers in this space know the vocabulary.

**Ongoing operating expenses.** ARRL's wording is broader than that heading: "Grant requests for
ongoing operations or expenses will not be considered." Site rent, insurance, internet service,
recurring licence fees and consumables all land inside it. Fund a thing, not a subscription.

**Individuals.** "Grants are awarded only to organizations, not individuals." Organizations only,
never to individuals — a club officer applying in their own name is applying in the wrong name.

There is also a restriction on where the work happens, and it is about the programme rather than
the applicant: "Only programs and initiatives conducted within the United States are eligible for
consideration."

## How much

"Awarded grants generally do not exceed $3,000." A starred sentence on the same page raises that
for the current year: "In support of ARRL's Year of the Club, award amounts may be up to $5,000 in
2026." Treat the higher figure as this year's ceiling, not as this year's expectation.

Ask for a number your line items justify:

> {{club.name}} ({{club.callsign}}) requests {{project.requestAmount}} toward a total project cost
> of {{project.budgetTotal}}.

ARRL also reserves the right to fund part of what you ask for — "The ARRL Foundation may elect to
fund a qualified proposal in its entirety or partially at its sole discretion" — so build a budget
that still describes a coherent project if it is trimmed. And plan for one bite: "It is rare for
the ARRL Foundation to award an extension to a previously issued grant."

## Co-funding is a stated preference

"The ARRL Foundation does not wish to be the sole funder of a proposal and gives preference to
groups that provide evidence of other successful fundraising conducted prior to proposal
submission." Note the tense: fundraising already conducted, not fundraising planned. Name the
other money in the first paragraph of the budget justification and quantify it.

> {{project.coFunder}} is contributing {{project.coFunderAmount}} toward this project.

If there is genuinely no co-funder, say what the club itself has already put in — dues spent,
equipment already owned, volunteer hours with an hourly value attached.

## Windows

Three windows: **February 1–28**, **June 1–30**, **October 1–31**. In ARRL's own words, "The ARRL
Foundation grants program accepts proposals on a cyclical model three times a year. Grant proposals
are accepted from:"

- February 1 - February 28
- June 1 - June 30
- October 1 - October 31

"Proposals will be reviewed by the Grant Committee at the close of each cycle", then go to the
full Foundation Board for a vote, and "Awardees will be notified approximately one month after the
closing of each cycle". Build that month into your schedule before you promise anyone a start
date.

## What the page says it prefers

Rather than guessing at reviewer psychology, use the preferences ARRL publishes:

- "Youth-based projects and initiatives are especially encouraged."
- "Proposals for projects with a high likelihood of sustainability or expansion are encouraged and
  preferred."
- "Proposals for projects with demonstrated support of the local amateur radio community or clubs
  are encouraged and preferred."
- The programme's own framing of its purpose: projects "with a focus on educating, licensing, and
  supporting Amateur Radio activities".

Each of those maps onto a component you are already writing: the outcomes table, the
sustainability section, and the letters or named partners in organizational capacity. A proposal
that answers all three in the funder's own terms is doing the reviewer's job for them.

## What the page does not say

The page publishes no AI policy: the captured page contains no mention of AI, ChatGPT or language
models in any form. GrantSpotter reports that as unaddressed rather than inferring a stance in
either direction. It also names no page limit and no required attachment list. Where you need a
ruling, the page gives an address: "Questions regarding grant proposals, eligibility, or
contributions to the ARRL Foundation may be directed by email to foundation@arrl.org."

## Overlay checklist

- [ ] Nothing in the request is emergency-communications equipment, facilities or a project
- [ ] Nothing in the request is an ongoing operation or expense
- [ ] The applicant is an organization, and the work happens in the United States
- [ ] Request justified line by line, and still coherent if it is funded in part
- [ ] Fundraising already conducted is named and quantified
- [ ] Sustainability or expansion argued, with local amateur radio support shown
- [ ] Submitted inside one of the three windows, with a month of notification time allowed
