## What ARISS awards

**No cash.** The proposal overview publishes no dollar figure anywhere on the page — no award size, no stipend, no reimbursement, no equipment budget. What is awarded is an event and the people who help you prepare for it:

> A scheduled ARISS contact is a voice-only communication via Amateur Radio between the International Space Station (ISS) crew and classrooms and communities.

The page puts the contact itself at "approximately 10 minutes in length", limited by "the radio communication window permitted by the logistics of orbital passes of the ISS".

The second half of the award is mentoring. ARISS's own process list says selected organizations attend an Orientation Session and are "paired with an ARISS Technical Mentor" — who helps develop an Equipment Plan — and an ARISS Educational Mentor. Only after "the Equipment Plan is approved" does the organization begin its Education Plan.

Budget accordingly. Every cost of hosting the contact is yours to cover from somewhere else, which is an argument for pairing this proposal with a club grant or a campus request, not an argument for skipping it. Nothing on the page says ARISS pays for anything, and this overlay does not tell you that it does.

## Who the page says may apply

> US schools and educational organizations may download the ARISS Proposal Form

and, of the contacts themselves:

> These scheduled contact opportunities are offered to formal and informal education institutions and organizations, individually or working together.

The applicant ARISS describes is an institution or an organization. A person applying alone is not the applicant named anywhere on this page. Colleges and universities are **not explicitly named** in either sentence, and the awarded contacts are dominated by school groups.

A collegiate club is not thereby excluded — "informal education institutions and organizations" is broad, and "working together" is an invitation to partner — but read what the page says it is looking for before deciding how to present yourself. It asks for "organizations that will draw large numbers of participants", and lists "potential audience size (we are looking to engage an entire school or organization, NOT just a single classroom)" among the evaluation criteria. So lead with the audience the contact reaches, name the partner institutions, and put your own membership second.

> {{club.name}} will host the contact at {{project.venue}} for {{project.beneficiaryCount}} students from {{club.institution}}, with {{team.instructorName}} leading the classroom sessions.

## The education plan is what is scored

The page states that "the ARISS program looks for organizations that will draw large numbers of participants and integrate the radio contact into a well-developed education plan", and then lists what proposals are evaluated on, in its own words:

- "the ability to integrate space and communication concepts into multiple areas of the curriculum for 4-6 months leading up to the contact"
- "how an ARISS contact will enhance the curriculum and experiences in the entire school or organization"
- "flexibility of the school or organization"
- "potential audience size (we are looking to engage an entire school or organization, NOT just a single classroom)"
- "community connections (ham radio groups or other community members who could be of assistance)"

One of those five is about the radio. The rest are about reach, curriculum and the people around you, so a proposal written as a station description is answering a question nobody asked. The page also says proposals "should include plans for students to" study "topics related to space technology, space exploration, or space research" and "learn about communication, wireless technology, and radio science" — the amateur radio content belongs inside the teaching, not beside it.

Write the curriculum out with dates, instructors and headcounts, running the months before the contact and continuing after it, and say who writes the students' questions and how they are chosen.

## Flexibility is a stated requirement, not a courtesy

> Because of the nature of human spaceflight and the complexity of scheduling activities aboard the ISS, organizations must demonstrate flexibility to accommodate changes in contact dates and times.

That is a requirement you must be able to show, so build a plan that survives a moved date: a curriculum anchored to a unit rather than to a single afternoon, and a named person who can reschedule the room and the audience. The page also gives the decision lag — "4 to 6 weeks after the close of the proposal window, organizations with approved proposals are sent a congratulatory email" — and puts the mentor pairing, the Equipment Plan and its approval between selection and the contact.

## The window

ARISS is one of the very few funders in this corpus that publishes its own cycle rather than leaving you to guess:

> Proposals are not accepted outside of the scheduled proposal windows.

The page says proposals are accepted during "four proposal windows each year", one each quarter, and the window sentence captured for this overlay reads:

> Proposal window opened July 1 and closes on September 30 for contacts to be held from January to June 2027.

That sentence is rewritten in place each quarter and prints no year for its opening date, so it is only true of the quarter it was read in. Read the live sentence before you plan against it; GrantSpotter records the date it last verified this page rather than presenting a remembered date as current.
