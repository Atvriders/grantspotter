## What this section has to do

Show every dollar as a line with a unit price, a quantity, a source, and a reason. A budget with round numbers and no unit prices reads as a guess, and a reviewer who thinks you guessed will assume you will overspend.

## Answer these before you write

1. What is the catalog or vendor price of each item today, and where did you get that figure?
2. What quantity, and why that quantity?
3. What is already paid for by somebody else?
4. Does this funder cap indirect costs, and at what percentage?
5. Does this funder refuse any of these categories outright?

Every figure in that table is yours to supply, and this template ships none of them on purpose. A number you did not look up is a number you cannot defend on a phone call with the funder, and the reviewer who catches one stops trusting the rest of the page.

## Cost share

Whether a match is required is the funder's fact, and the published answer is often no answer at all. Three states, never two: the funder requires a match, the funder has said it does not, or the material is silent. Silence is not permission to write "no match is required" — read the funder's own page, and where it says nothing, ask and record who told you.

## Justification narrative

> One short paragraph per non-obvious line. Explain the quantity, not the item. "Two antennas" needs a reason; "an antenna" usually does not.

## Common failure

Round numbers. A line reading "equipment" with no unit price behind it tells a reviewer you have not priced anything. Quote the prices you actually found, name the vendor and the date beside each one, and let the total land on whatever odd figure the arithmetic produces — a total ending in three zeros reads as an estimate no matter how carefully you arrived at it.
