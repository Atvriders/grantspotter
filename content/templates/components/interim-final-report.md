## What this section has to do

Report what you promised against what happened, in that order, using the same numbers you used in the application.

Nothing in this template knows how your project went, and nothing in it will guess. Every figure below is a hole you fill from a record you can produce: a logbook, a sign-in sheet, a receipt, a licence database entry, a repeater's own uptime. This document is read by the person who decides whether to fund you again, so a number nobody can source is worse than a number that came in low.

Check the funder's published reporting requirement before you write. Some awards carry one and some do not, and where a funder publishes a form, a period, or a due date, theirs wins over this shape.

## Answer these before you write

1. Which numbers did you promise? Copy them from your outcomes table verbatim, including the ones you would rather leave out.
2. What are the actual numbers today, and where did each one come from?
3. Where a number came in low, what happened, and what did you do about it?
4. What did the money actually buy, against the budget lines you submitted?
5. What obligation remains open — an on-air period, an open-source release, a follow-up report?

> Quote anything still owed from the funder's own published page or from your award letter, and give a link or a date for the document you are quoting. An obligation nobody published is not an obligation, and reporting against an invented one tells a funder something untrue about their own program.

Which of those apply to you is the funder's statement to make, not this template's.

## Common failure

Reporting activity instead of outcomes, because activity is easier to describe. If the application promised twenty-four licensed operators, the report opens with the number of licensed operators — the actual one, whatever it turned out to be.

The second failure is the summary sentence that grades itself — the one declaring that the project delivered what it set out to. That is a claim, not a result, and a reader holding the table above it does not need the claim. Give the counts and let them do the grading.
