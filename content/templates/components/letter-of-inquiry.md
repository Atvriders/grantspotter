## What this section has to do

Ask a funder, before you write a full proposal, whether the project is worth their time. One page. A letter of inquiry that runs to three pages has already answered its own question.

This letter goes to a named person who will remember what it said, and it goes out over your signature. Everything in it that is a fact — a name, a title, a date, an amount, a place, a count — is yours to supply and yours to stand behind. What follows is the shape only.

## Before you write

1. **Who is it addressed to, and what is their title?** Find the person. A generic salutation tells the reader you did not look, and a wrong title tells them you looked carelessly.
2. **Does this funder invite letters of inquiry at all?** Some publish a full application and no inquiry step. Sending an unasked-for letter costs you the first impression.
3. **What is the one number you want them to remember?** Decide it now, put it in paragraph four, and do not bury it.

## Structure

**Paragraph 1 — who and how much.** {{club.name}} ({{club.callsign}}) requests {{project.requestAmount}} from {{funder.name}} for {{project.title}}.

**Paragraph 2 — the problem, in two sentences.** {{project.problem}}

**Paragraph 3 — what you will do and by when.** {{project.summary}} The work runs from {{project.startDate}} to {{project.endDate}} at {{project.venue}}.

**Paragraph 4 — what will be true afterwards.** One countable outcome, with the number and the date. A count and a date is an outcome; "increased interest in radio" is not one, and neither is "a stronger club".

**Paragraph 5 — why this funder.** One specific sentence tying the project to something this funder has actually published or actually funded — name it, and say where you read it. If you cannot write the sentence without flattery, delete the paragraph; a funder can tell the difference between research and praise.

**Close.** Name the next step and give a date. {{team.leadName}} is the person who will send the full proposal and the person they should reply to. Write the date you can meet, not the date you wish you could.

## Common failure

Restating the funder's mission back at them. They wrote it; they know it. Spend the space on your own numbers instead — the callsign, the count, the model number, the room the work happens in.
