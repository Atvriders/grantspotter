## What this section has to do

Make a selection committee remember one specific person. Committees read stacks of essays that could have been written by anyone holding a licence. The ones they remember contain a scene, a number, and a plan.

This is the one component addressed to an individual applicant rather than to a club, and every fact in it is a fact about you. Nothing here fills any of them in.

## Answer these before you write

1. What is the single most specific thing you have done with a radio? Give the date, the band or mode, the equipment by model, and what happened — including the part that did not work.
2. What did you build, fix, or break, and what did you learn from it?
3. Who taught you, by name and callsign, and what did they actually say?
4. What do you intend to do with your field of study after you graduate, stated concretely enough that somebody could check in five years?
5. What does this award change, materially? Name the thing it pays for.

## Structure

**Opening — a scene, not a thesis.** Start inside a specific moment: a date, a place, a piece of equipment, a person. Do not open with a definition of amateur radio; assume the committee already knows what it is.

**Middle — the through-line.** Give your own facts once, plainly, then stop repeating them. If you hold a licence, state it exactly: {{student.name}} ({{student.callsign}}), {{student.licenseClass}}, first licensed {{student.licensedSince}}, studying {{student.fieldOfStudy}} at {{student.institution}}. If you are not licensed yet, say which class you are studying for and when you sit the exam — a dated plan is a fact too, and an honest "not yet" beats a vague "involved in". Then connect the scene to the field of study with events, not adjectives.

**Middle — evidence.** Two or three things you have actually completed, each with a date and a count. A contest score. A repeater you helped put back on the air, and the model of what failed. A licence class you taught, and how many people passed.

**Close — the plan and the ask.** What you intend to do after {{student.gradYear}}, and what this award makes possible that is otherwise not. Be specific about the money: it pays tuition, or a term of housing, or a particular instrument. "Support my education" is not an answer.

## Common failure

The essay that says amateur radio taught the writer discipline, teamwork, and problem-solving. Three abstractions, no scene, interchangeable with every other essay in the stack. Replace all three with one story that contains a date and a piece of equipment.

The second failure is quieter: stretching. A committee that catches one exaggerated claim re-reads everything else looking for more. Write the smaller true thing.
